# All You Need to Know About Network Design & Implementation

## **All you need to know about Network Design & Implementation.**
Although many businesses know that they require Network Design & Implementation within their business, not many people understand the processes behind designing and implementing a network.
This article is a breakdown of the steps behind network design and implementation.
We start by getting to know the company and gathering all the necessary information in the consultation phase, such as your future plans regarding your network, including endpoints, users, devices, and other network elements. Your Network may need to be built from scratch. Knowing valuable information helps us build the bigger picture and lets us create a plan for establishing further steps.

## **Designing stage**
With [](http://dev.network-consultancy.com/services/network-design-and-deployment)[network design services,](https://network-consultancy.com/services/network-design-and-deployment) we use all of the valuable information about your business; our work goes further into creating a network diagram. A network diagram is a visual representation of the network's components, their connections, and the flow of information between them.
Typically, the components are represented as boxes, with arrows showing the connections between them and the flow of information. Network diagrams are used to identify and analyse network requirements, design solutions, and troubleshoot network issues. They can also be used to improve communication between network users and administrators, helping everyone to understand the network better.
Building the network from scratch requires an extra step in assembling all the points. In comparison, with an existing network environment, we will include the current infrastructure, which will be kept running during the installation of the network.

## **Network Implementation**
The installation of IT networks typically involves the setup and configuration of network hardware, such as routers, switches, and access points, as well as the installation of cabling and wiring infrastructure. Additionally, the installation of the appropriate software components, such as operating systems and applications, may be necessary. Finally, the configuration of the network, including the setup of security protocols, is essential for the successful functioning of the network. Having highly skilled engineers with good and recognised accreditation will help ensure that the processes are running smoothly. Before the network goes live, the network team will also provide comprehensive testing and quality assurance to ensure that the network is functioning properly before going live. After that, depending on which company you run with, you should receive ongoing support and maintenance, ensuring your network is always running optimally.

## Network Testing and Optimisation
Before the network is handed over, every component should be thoroughly tested to ensure it performs as expected. This includes checking network speed, reliability, security, wireless coverage and device connectivity.
Engineers will typically test:
*   Internet and internal network performance
*   Wired and wireless connections
*   Firewall and security settings
*   VLAN configuration
*   Remote access and VPN connectivity
*   Network redundancy and failover
*   Device compatibility
Any issues identified during testing are resolved before the network becomes fully operational, helping to minimise disruption once users begin working.

## Network Documentation and Handover
Once implementation is complete, comprehensive documentation should be provided. This ensures your internal IT team or managed service provider has everything needed to maintain and support the network.
Documentation may include:
*   Network topology diagrams
*   IP address schedules
*   Device inventory
*   Configuration backups
*   Administrator credentials (where appropriate)
*   Support and warranty information
Keeping documentation up to date makes future upgrades, troubleshooting and disaster recovery much easier.

## Ongoing Network Support and Maintenance
A network is not something that should be installed and forgotten. Regular maintenance helps keep your infrastructure secure, reliable and performing at its best.
Typical maintenance services include:
*   Software and firmware updates
*   Security patch management
*   Performance monitoring
*   Hardware health checks
*   Capacity planning
*   Backup verification
*   Fault diagnosis and resolution
Proactive monitoring can often identify potential issues before they affect your business operations.

## Planning for Future Growth
Your network should be designed with future expansion in mind. As your business grows, your infrastructure should be capable of supporting additional users, devices, cloud services and new office locations without requiring a complete redesign.
A scalable network allows you to:
*   Add new employees with ease
*   Support hybrid and remote working
*   Integrate cloud applications
*   Increase wireless coverage
*   Expand to multiple sites.
*   Adopt new technologies as your business evolves.
Planning ahead can reduce future costs and minimise disruption.

# Key Elements of a Resilient Network

# Title - [Key Elements of a Resilient Network](https://network-consultancy.com/resources/blogs/resilient-network-design)

**Date Published: 15/07/2026**

## **Introduction**

Resilience can be defined as the ability of an organisation to quickly recover from adversity or setback. In simple words, it is the ability to get back on your feet after you fall. However, with everything happening on computer networks, how do you design resilience into your corporate environment?

Despite technology stacks demanding more frequent updates, Information Technology Consulting (ITIC) reported that 85 per cent of major corporations now demand an uptime minimum of at least 99.9 per cent for critical hardware, operating systems, and main line of business (LOB) applications.

## In this article, we look at four factors that will help build a resilient network.
### 1\. Know That Everything Fails
The first step to building a resilient network is understanding everything that fails, from routers to switches, cables, circuits, and even cross-connects. Hence, regular network maintenance is crucial to smooth business operations. This will help keep your system at the right software levels, allow the application of [network security](http://network-consultancy.com/services/network-consultancy) patches, and even give way for hardware maintenance and replacement.
### 2\. Operating Hours
Next, you should think about and determine the operating hours of the environment. For instance, your office network might not have users after work hours or during the weekends. On the other hand, this sort of network might come with strict reliability and availability requirements during regular hours. Nonetheless, it can be maintained after work hours too. On the other hand, data centres or life and safety systems should operate 24/7. Hence, the right design is essential for these networks to deal with failures as well as the ability to operate during maintenance.
### 3\. Virtualisation, Cloud, and Saas Applications
Then come virtualisation, cloud, and [SaaS application](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Software_as_a_service) suites. Often people feel cloud-based applications lie outside IT controls. But this is not really the truth. For instance, AWS makes a considerable effort to advise clients on the availability offered by applications. In addition, applications put forward several service-level agreements to users based on where they are hosted, like with single availability zones, multiple availability zones, or operating across regions. It is also important how enterprises and their customers connect to the cloud or SaaS providers.
### 4\. Reliable Remote Connectivity
In the post-pandemic era, if there is something enterprises should think of, that is the reliability of their [secure remote access solutions'](http://network-consultancy.com/services/remote-access-solution) connectivity. For example, does the connectivity run on primary or secondary VPN connectors? Is it load-balanced across systems, allowing for required maintenance?
## How Important Is Resilience?
Healthcare organisations, financial institutions, and government public safety agencies engaged in time-sensitive computing operations are just a few of the many types of entities that can’t accept even a few hours of downtime compared to their counterparts in the corporate world. For this reason, a resilient network sounds more important to them than ever in the era of technology and the internet. Typically, the IT resilience strategies employed in a data centre will vary from one organisation to another depending on the workloads and application availability.

Organisations with more mission-specific workloads will have to introduce greater resiliency techniques at greater levels within a data centre. Why? Because the cost of not appropriately protecting critical computing services happens to be more expensive than an unplanned outage or fallout. On the contrary, non-essential workloads that can do with a few disruptions can receive less resiliency or remain offline until they are restored completely.

The rule of thumb here is to carefully determine the systems, services, and applications crucial to your business viability and those less essential. If you take a quick glance, you might think that email is a simple, non-essential business function when it is not. If the employees of an eCommerce business or B2B company cannot communicate with its customers or partners for an extended period, the results could be disastrous.

## Frequently Asked Questions

### 1\. What is a resilient network, and why is it important?
A resilient network is designed to recover quickly from failures and minimise downtime. It helps businesses maintain critical operations, improve reliability, and ensure essential applications and services remain available even during unexpected disruptions or maintenance.

### 2\. Why should businesses assume that network components can fail?
Every part of a network, including routers, switches, cables, and circuits, can experience failures. Planning for these possibilities through proactive maintenance, software updates, and hardware replacements helps reduce the risk of prolonged outages.

### 3\. How do operating hours affect network resilience planning?
Different environments have different availability requirements. Office networks may allow maintenance outside business hours, while data centres and mission-critical systems often require 24/7 uptime and must be designed to remain operational during maintenance or failures.

### 4\. What role do cloud and SaaS applications play in network resilience?
Cloud and SaaS platforms can support resilience through features such as multiple availability zones and regional redundancy. However, organisations must also ensure reliable connectivity and understand the service levels offered by their providers.

### 5\. Why is reliable remote connectivity essential for modern businesses?
With remote and hybrid working now common, dependable VPN connections and load-balanced remote access systems help employees stay securely connected while enabling maintenance and reducing the impact of network disruptions.

# Wireless vs Wired Networks

# Title - [Wireless vs Wired Networks](https://network-consultancy.com/resources/blogs/wireless-vs-wired-networks)

**Date Published: 12/7/2026**

# **Wireless vs Wired Networks**
In the age of modern IT, there’s no right or wrong choice when it comes to wired vs wireless networks. Both types of networks have their advantages and disadvantages, and the final choice comes down to what is best for your business. Hybrid working is huge at the moment, and it’s set to become even more popular. This means that wireless networks may also rise in popularity, due to how they enhance mobility; however, this may not be the case for businesses that work strictly from the office.
Let’s take a closer look at some of the pros and cons of these kinds of networks.
## **Advantages of Wireless Networks**
**Cost:** The cost of a wireless network is generally far less than its wired counterpart. Typically, wireless network setup requires fewer switches, cables, routers and general hardware, which makes it cheaper from the get-go. Furthermore, these kinds of networks are usually set up more quickly than wired networks. These things together mean that they’re relatively inexpensive from the outset.
**BYOD and Hybrid Working:** In the age of hybrid and home working, it’s more common for workplaces to implement a BYOD (Bring Your Own Device) policy. With a wireless network, this is easier to implement as it means that employees can seamlessly connect to the network with their devices without having to worry about connecting via wires and cables.
**Mobility:** With a wireless network, employees can connect from anywhere in the office. This boosts productivity as there’s no need to wait around for something to download at a desk. Work can move easily from the desk to the boardroom and beyond. Wireless networks facilitate collaboration as teams don’t have to rely on working within the constraints of cables to stay online.
## **Disadvantages of Wireless Networks**
**Speed:** In general, wireless networks are slower than their wired counterpart. This is because the network is dependent on the maximum speed capacity of your configuration, and many devices connecting at the same time can often slow things down. This can be fixed by using a hybrid solution that uses both wired and wireless networks.
**Reliability:** Signal speed and strength can vary when using a wireless network. They can be prone to interference, and this is what can cause the variation in signal. Furthermore, dead spots and obstructions can be an issue. Although wireless networks can facilitate movement and collaboration within a team, it’s important to be aware of any potential downfalls in your location that could cause a sketchy signal.
**Security:** Without proper practices in place, wireless networks can be less secure than wired networks. This is because as signals get transmitted through the air, they are easier to intercept. Although this seems worrying, with encryption technology, firewalls and proper training in place, the benefits outweigh the risks, and wireless networks can still be used safely within a business.
## **Advantages of Wired Networks**
**Reliability:** When installed correctly, a wired network is one of the most reliable and stable network types. They don’t suffer interference in the same way as a wireless network, and connectivity issues are far rarer.
**Visibility:** Unlike wireless networks, wired networks are not visible to devices on other networks. It’s this visibility (or lack thereof) that makes wired networks safer and less likely to suffer interference. Furthermore, communication between devices on the network is quick and simple, and grants network admins a high level of control so that the network remains safe continually.
**Speed:** Wired networks are generally the fastest type of network, and they’re significantly faster than their wireless counterparts. Due to each device being connected via a separate cable, users don’t have to worry about connectivity and getting bogged down due to the volume of people on the network. Furthermore, wired networks have a limited and controlled set of users; in other words, they don’t take on more than they can handle.
## **Disadvantages of Wired Networks**
**Mobility:** Unlike a wireless network, which allows you to move and work freely, a wired network does not offer the same level of mobility. Generally, they’re quite inflexible, so moving locations can prove tricky. If you do need to move, often the only option is to run extra cables and install new switches, which can be inconvenient and time-consuming.
**Installation:** As there is more hardware to install, a wired network can be an inconvenience when it is brand new. Installation can take a while depending on the size of your network and the number of components that are required. However, this is not an issue after installation, and unless there is major work to be done, you don’t have to deal with it regularly.
**Maintenance:** Smaller networks may not need a server, but larger networks tend to. This increases the need for [network support and maintenance](http://network-consultancy.com/services/network-support) to ensure that everything runs as it should. At least to begin with, this can also be inconvenient. There is a solution though, and managed network providers can take over the maintenance and installation for you, lessening the disruptions.

Businesses should consider their requirements for speed, mobility, security, reliability, and budget. Wireless networks offer flexibility and support hybrid working, while wired networks provide greater speed, stability, and security.
### 2\. Why are wireless networks popular in hybrid working environments?
Wireless networks allow employees to connect easily from different locations within the office using laptops, tablets, and personal devices. This flexibility supports collaboration and productivity in hybrid and remote working setups.
### 3\. Are wired networks more reliable than wireless networks?
Yes, wired networks are generally more reliable because they are not affected by signal interference, dead zones, or wireless congestion. They provide consistent connectivity and stable performance for business-critical applications.
### 4\. What are the main security differences between wired and wireless networks?
Wired networks are typically more secure because they require physical access to connect. Wireless networks can be more vulnerable to interception, but modern encryption, firewalls, and security policies can significantly reduce these risks.

Yes, many organisations choose a hybrid network approach. Combining wired and wireless networks enables businesses to benefit from the speed and reliability of wired connections while maintaining the flexibility and mobility of wireless access.

## What Is Wireless Security?
Essentially, wireless security helps prevent unwanted users from accessing a specific wireless network. Also known as Wi-Fi security, wireless security aims to keep data secure by making it only accessible to authorised users.
## How Does Wireless Security Work?
Wireless Security Protocols like [**Wired Equivalent Privacy**](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wired_Equivalent_Privacy) (WEP) and [**Wi-Fi Protected Access**](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wi-Fi_Protected_Access) (WPA) are developed by authentication security protocols put forth by the Wireless Alliance to ensure wireless security. Generally, wireless security protocols are of four types:
*   Wired Equivalent Privacy (WEP)
*   Wi-Fi Protected Access (WPA)
*   Wi-Fi Protected Access 2 (WPA 2)
*   Wi-Fi Protected Access 3 (WPA 3)
The first step to securing your network is identifying the network yours falls under.
## 5 Wireless Security Solutions
The external and internal policies, security, and management [**network design and deployment**](http://network-consultancy.com/services/network-design-and-deployment) you have in place should offer top-notch security and possess the flexibility to adapt to changing threats. Mentioned below are a few wireless security solutions, of which some are fit for any networking environment while others are designed to address wireless security threats. By adopting a comprehensive approach, your organisation will be able to protect its wireless network from intruders.

### 1\. Firewalls
A quality firewall is like a strong security foundation for your organisation that will prevent unauthorised access while offering secure network availability for your on-site as well as remote staff, business partners, and customers. Currently, firewalls happen to be a security staple in securing almost all networking environments, both wired and wireless.
### 2\. Content Filtering
Just like the previous solution, content filtering is essential in all network environments as it helps protect your internal activity. In addition, filtering and monitoring software will keep your employees safe from accessing content through the internet that could potentially harm your operations.
### 3\. Intrusion Detection
Another solution ideal for wired and wireless networks, intrusion detection and prevention software offers the necessary software intelligence that will help your network quickly identify and halt attacks, worms, threats, viruses, and more.
### 4\. Authentication
As the name suggests, authentication and identification methods will secure the data on your network. Alongside password protection, solutions like key fobs and biometric authentication also ensure that only people with authority can access secure data, thereby keeping your wireless network safe.
### 5\. Data Encryption
Currently, the business climate depends on gathering, analysing, and sharing vital information about your organisation and its customers. Data encryption safeguards the wireless networks, Virtual Private Networks, and Secure Socket Layers you use to share data.

Securing your wireless network should be a top priority to keep your data safe and secure. The solutions mentioned in this article are easy to put into practice even if you are a non-tech-savvy person.

## What Is Network Validation?
Network validation is the process of checking, analysing, and confirming that your network infrastructure is working securely, reliably, and as expected.
In simple terms, network validation ensures that your business network can be trusted. It helps you understand whether your wireless network, devices, applications, and security controls are operating correctly and identifies potential issues before they become serious problems.
Modern businesses rely heavily on their networks for daily operations, cloud applications, customer data, communication systems, and remote working. A network failure can lead to downtime, security risks, productivity loss, and financial impact.
This is why businesses are moving away from reactive network management and adopting a proactive approach through continuous network validation.
Instead of waiting for a problem to happen, network validation allows organisations to:
*   Identify weaknesses before they cause disruption.
*   Predict possible failures
*   Understand unusual network behaviour.
*   Improve network performance
*   Strengthen security controls
*   Reduce unexpected downtime
*   Maintain a reliable user experience.

## Why Is Network Validation Important for Businesses?
Many IT teams still follow a reactive approach when managing network security. This usually means waiting until a problem occurs, investigating the cause, applying a fix, and moving on.

*   Network traffic patterns

## Wireless Network Authentication Methods Explained
Wireless authentication is one of the most important elements of network validation. It controls who can access your network and ensures that unauthorised users cannot connect.
The three main wireless authentication methods include:
### Open Authentication
Open authentication is the simplest wireless security method.
It only requires a device to know the network name (SSID) before connecting. While this makes access easy, it provides limited security because anyone who knows the SSID may attempt to connect.
This method is generally unsuitable for businesses handling sensitive information.
* * *
### Shared Authentication
Shared authentication uses a common security key that is shared between users and devices.
The device must provide the correct key before access is granted.
This method is commonly used by:
*   Small businesses
*   Home networks
*   Smaller office environments
However, shared passwords can become a security risk if they are widely distributed or not updated regularly.
* * *
### EAP (Extensible Authentication Protocol)
EAP authentication is commonly used by larger organisations and enterprises.
It works by connecting devices to an authentication server that verifies user credentials before granting network access.
Benefits include:
*   Individual user authentication
*   Better access control
*   Improved security monitoring
*   Easier user management
Enterprise environments often combine EAP authentication with technologies such as WPA3 Enterprise and RADIUS servers for stronger wireless security.
* * *
## Network Validation and Zero Trust Security
Modern businesses are increasingly adopting Zero Trust security principles.
Zero Trust follows the approach of:
“Never trust, always verify.”
Instead of automatically trusting users or devices inside the network, every connection request is continuously checked.
Network validation supports Zero Trust by helping businesses:
*   Monitor connected devices
*   Verify user access permissions.
*   Identify unusual activity
*   Reduce unauthorised access risks.
*   Improve security visibility
For organisations with remote workers, cloud applications, and multiple locations, combining network validation with Zero Trust principles creates a stronger security framework.
