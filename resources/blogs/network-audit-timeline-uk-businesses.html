How Long Does a Network Audit Take? A Complete Guide to UK Businesses

A network audit should never be measured by speed alone because the value comes from uncovering risks before they become business disruptions.

What Determines How Long a Network Audit Takes

There is no universal answer to how long a network audit takes. A small office with a handful of switches and users can often be assessed within a single working day. In contrast, a large enterprise with multiple sites, cloud environments and complex security policies may require several weeks.

Professional audits are designed to provide far more than a quick health check. They analyse infrastructure performance, identify security weaknesses, validate configurations, review resilience, and recommend improvements that support future growth. Businesses investing in network consulting solutions should expect an audit to be thorough rather than rushed.

Several factors influence the duration, including the number of connected devices, wireless infrastructure, remote workers, cloud services, legacy hardware and regulatory requirements. A structured audit also includes interviews with stakeholders, documentation reviews and detailed reporting, all of which contribute to the overall timeframe.

Typical Network Audit Times for UK Businesses

The following estimates represent average durations. Actual timelines vary depending on infrastructure complexity and business requirements.

Business SizeEstimated Time
Small office (10–25 users)1 day
SME (25–100 users)2–4 days
Multi-site organisation1–2 weeks
Enterprise2–4 weeks

These figures are averages rather than guarantees. A well-documented SME with modern equipment may complete an audit faster than expected, while a smaller organisation with outdated infrastructure may require additional investigation.

Factors That Increase Audit Duration

Number of Users

The more users connected to the network, the more devices, permissions and traffic patterns require assessment. Organisations with hundreds of users often generate significantly larger datasets for performance and security analysis.

Multiple Office Locations

Businesses operating across Birmingham, London, the West Midlands or multiple UK regions require engineers to review WAN connectivity, branch office configurations and communication links between sites. Each location adds complexity and testing requirements.

Cloud Infrastructure

Modern businesses increasingly rely on cloud platforms, requiring auditors to examine both on-premises and cloud environments. This commonly includes:

  • Microsoft 365
  • Azure
  • AWS
  • Hybrid cloud deployments

Each platform introduces additional identity management, security policies, networking configurations and integration points that require assessment.

Network Documentation Quality

Well-maintained documentation dramatically speeds up an audit. Useful documentation includes:

  • Network diagrams
  • IP address schedules
  • Device inventories
  • Configuration backups
  • Change records

Where documentation is incomplete or outdated, auditors must spend additional time manually discovering infrastructure.

Legacy Equipment

Older routers, switches and firewalls frequently require manual verification because they may lack modern management tools or updated firmware. Legacy infrastructure often increases troubleshooting time and can expose compatibility or performance issues that warrant further investigation.

Wireless Networks

Large wireless deployments require extensive coverage testing. Auditors typically evaluate:

  • Signal strength
  • Roaming performance
  • Channel interference
  • Dead zones
  • Capacity during peak usage

Remote Workforce

Hybrid and remote working models increase audit scope. VPN performance, remote authentication methods, endpoint security and remote access policies all require validation to ensure employees remain productive without compromising security.

Cyber Security Review

Security assessments often represent one of the longest parts of a professional audit. This includes reviewing:

  • Firewalls
  • Switches
  • VPN configurations
  • Access controls
  • Endpoint security

For example, an audit may examine over 50 firewall rules, assess administrator privileges and identify outdated firmware that could expose the organisation to cyber threats.

What Happens During Each Stage of the Audit

A structured audit generally follows several clearly defined stages.

Discovery — Approximate duration: 2–6 hours

Consultants gather business objectives, review existing documentation and interview key stakeholders to understand operational requirements.

Device Inventory — Approximate duration: Half day to two days

This stage commonly involves analysing 100+ connected devices, including switches, routers, wireless access points, printers, servers and endpoints. Auditors verify firmware versions, identify unknown devices and document hardware lifecycles.

Performance Testing — Approximate duration: 4–8 hours

Engineers measure:

  • Network latency
  • Packet loss
  • Bandwidth utilisation
  • Application performance
  • Internet connectivity

Security Assessment — Approximate duration: 1–3 days

Typical activities include:

  • Reviewing 50+ firewall rules
  • Testing VPN configurations
  • Validating administrator permissions
  • Examining access controls
  • Identifying outdated firmware
  • Reviewing patch management

Bandwidth Analysis — Approximate duration: Several hours

Traffic monitoring identifies excessive utilisation, unnecessary applications and opportunities to improve performance.

Wi-Fi Testing — Approximate duration: Half day to several days

Engineers test Wi-Fi signal strength across every office floor, identify interference and verify user experience throughout the premises.

Reporting — Approximate duration: 1–2 days

All findings are documented into a comprehensive report covering risks, performance issues, security concerns and improvement priorities.

Recommendations — Approximate duration: Several hours

Consultants present prioritised actions, explain business impact and recommend phased improvements aligned with budgets and future growth.

Common Delays Businesses Can Avoid

Several avoidable issues frequently extend audit times. These include:

  • Poor documentation
  • Unknown devices
  • Outdated firmware
  • Unmanaged switches
  • No asset inventory
  • Old network diagrams
  • Missing administrator credentials

Preparing these items beforehand enables auditors to focus on identifying improvements rather than gathering missing information.

How to Reduce Audit Time While Improving Results

Although infrastructure complexity cannot always be simplified, businesses can significantly reduce audit duration through careful preparation.

Prepare Documentation Before the Audit

Gathering key information beforehand enables engineers to begin technical analysis immediately. Recommended documents include:

  • Network diagrams
  • ISP details
  • Firewall policies
  • Asset inventory
  • Administrator's credentials
  • Recent infrastructure changes

Accurate documentation reduces unnecessary investigation and improves report accuracy.

Choose an Experienced Network Specialist

Experienced consultants use proven methodologies, automated discovery tools and structured reporting to deliver consistent results. A trusted network management company provides expertise across infrastructure, cyber security and compliance while offering ongoing network support after the audit has been completed.

Professional specialists also understand network design services, enabling recommendations that support both current performance and future expansion rather than simply identifying problems.

  • Updated network topology diagram
  • Capacity planning guidance
  • Compliance findings
  • Prioritised improvement roadmap
  • Budget estimates

These deliverables enable decision-makers to plan improvements confidently and allocate investment where they deliver the greatest business value.

When Should UK Businesses Schedule a Network Audit?

Businesses should consider scheduling an audit during key periods of organisational change, including:

  • Before office relocation
  • Before cloud migration
  • After cyber incidents
  • Before Cyber Essentials certification
  • Annual infrastructure reviews
  • Following mergers or acquisitions
  • Before opening a new branch
  • Before large Wi-Fi upgrades
  • Before replacing servers
  • Before implementing an IT network installation service

Across Birmingham, London, the West Midlands and other UK SMEs, regular audits help organisations remain resilient while supporting growth, compliance and operational continuity.
